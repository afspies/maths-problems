Rectangular pegs from Young-regular Jordan curves

A finite-variation bridge to the Asano–Ike criterion

partial result problems/square-peg · 2026-07-24 · unrestricted conjecture open

Abstract. We combine Asano–Ike’s 2026 smooth-approximation criterion with Boedihardjo–Geng’s embedded polygon interpolation and Young integration. Every Jordan parametrization of finite p-variation, p<2, inscribes every prescribed rectangle. A Dini–Young condition reaches the critical scale. An explicit double spiral proves that the class includes curves that are neither rectifiable nor locally monotone.

1 · Main theorem

Theorem. Let c:S1→ℝ2 be a Jordan parametrization of finite p-variation for some 1≤p<2. Then c(S1) inscribes a θ-rectangle for every θ∈(0,π), including a square.
Critical extension. The conclusion also holds if the periodic coordinate moduli satisfy ∫01ωx(r)ωy(r)r−2dr<∞.

2 · Why the approximation is genuinely embedded

Choose p<q<2. Boedihardjo–Geng construct arbitrarily fine affine interpolants that remain Jordan and converge in q-variation. Each finite polygon is rounded inside pairwise disjoint vertex disks, on the same parameter circle, with an arbitrarily small change in 1-variation. No convolution of the original rough curve is assumed to remain embedded.

For c=(x,y), Young–Lœve continuity gives

Fn(t)=∫0tyndxn  → ∫0ty dx.

The period increments converge too, so the primitives converge locally uniformly on ℝ. This is precisely Asano–Ike Theorem 1.1.

3 · A strict nonrectifiable witness

For 1<d<2, join two rotated spirals

γ0(θ)=θ−1/deiθ,   γ1(θ)=eiδγ0(θ),   θ≥1,

by an outer circular arc. Monotone radius proves embeddedness. Its length diverges like Σn−1/d, but its p-variation is finite for every p>d. Every projection oscillates infinitely often at the common origin, so it is not locally monotone.

4 · The critical p=2 obstruction

Uniform convergence plus bounded 2-variation does not determine area: a circle of radius n−1/2 traversed n times shrinks uniformly to a point but retains constant signed area with multiplicity. This is not a Jordan counterexample; it identifies the missing geometric rough-path second level. The Dini condition above is a positive critical subclass.
