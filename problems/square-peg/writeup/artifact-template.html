Rectangular pegs from Young-regular Jordan curves

A finite-variation bridge to the Asano–Ike criterion

partial result problems/square-peg 2026-07-24 unrestricted conjecture open
Abstract We combine Asano–Ike’s 2026 smooth-approximation criterion with Boedihardjo–Geng’s embedded polygon interpolation and Young integration. Every Jordan parametrization of finite p-variation, p < 2, inscribes every prescribed rectangle. A Dini–Young hypothesis reaches the critical scale. An explicit double spiral shows that the class includes curves that are neither rectifiable nor locally monotone.

1 · Result

Theorem 1. Let c: S1 → ℝ2 be a Jordan parametrization of finite p-variation for some 1 ≤ p < 2. Then c(S1) inscribes a θ-rectangle for every θ ∈ (0,π), including a square.

2 · Proof architecture

Choose p < q < 2. Boedihardjo–Geng construct arbitrarily fine affine interpolants that remain Jordan and converge in q-variation. Each finite polygon is rounded inside pairwise disjoint vertex disks, on the same parameter circle, with an arbitrarily small change in 1-variation. Embeddedness is therefore proved, not assumed.

Young–Lœve continuity gives uniform convergence of the normalized Liouville primitives on one period. Their period increments converge too, so the primitives converge locally uniformly on ℝ. These are exactly the hypotheses of Asano–Ike Theorem 1.1.

3 · Strict nonrectifiable witness

For 1 < d < 2, take two rotated spirals

γ0(θ)=θ−1/deiθ,   γ1(θ)=eiδγ0(θ),   θ≥1, (2)

and join their radius-one endpoints by a circular arc. Monotone radius proves embeddedness. Length diverges like Σn−1/d, while p-variation is finite for every p > d. Every linear projection oscillates infinitely often at the common origin, so the curve is not locally monotone.

4 · Critical p = 2 frontier

Uniform convergence plus bounded 2-variation does not determine signed area: a circle of radius n−1/2 traversed n times shrinks uniformly to a point, retains bounded 2-variation, and has constant area with multiplicity. This is not a Jordan counterexample; it isolates the missing geometric rough-path second level. The Dini condition above is a positive critical subclass.

5 · Exact hygiene checks

The finite harness does not prove the analytic theorem. It checks rational polygon simplicity, Liouville/shoelace primitives under subdivision, and candidate squares exactly.
