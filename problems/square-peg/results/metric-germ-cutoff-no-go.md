# The zero-Hofer diagonal-germ trap

## The tempting argument

Write
\[
 Q_s=(\phi\times\phi)^{-1}R_s(\phi\times\phi),\qquad
 H^\phi(z,w)=\frac{|\phi(z)-\phi(w)|^2}{4}.
\]
The topological Hamiltonian \(H^\phi\) is non-negative, is invariant under
its own flow, and vanishes on the diagonal.  If \(h_\varepsilon\) agrees with
the identity near \(0\) and is constant above \(3\varepsilon\), then—after a
compact midpoint cut-off—the flow \(Q^\varepsilon\) generated by
\(h_\varepsilon(H^\phi)\)

- agrees with \(Q\) on an invariant tube of the diagonal; and
- has Hofer oscillation \(O(\varepsilon)\).

Consequently
\[
 d(Q^\varepsilon F_0,F_0)=O(\varepsilon)                 \tag{1}
\]
in the Tamarkin interleaving metric.  It is tempting to combine locality
with (1) and conclude that the diagonal microlocal germ of \(QF_0\) is the
standard one.

That conclusion is invalid.

## Exact persistence countermodel

Fix an action \(a_0\) and let \(V_{\rm std}\) be any constructible
persistence object.  Put
\[
 V_\varepsilon
 =V_{\rm std}\oplus\mathbb k_{[a_0,a_0+\varepsilon)}.
\]
Then
\[
 d(V_\varepsilon,V_{\rm std})\leq\varepsilon,\qquad
 V_\varepsilon\longrightarrow V_{\rm std},               \tag{2}
\]
but the microstalk at the fixed left endpoint \(a_0\) contains an extra copy
of \(\mathbb k\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).  The extra bar disappears in
the metric limit while its endpoint microstalk never vanishes.

There is an even sharper ordinary-sheaf model.  The action skyscraper
\[
 S=\mathbb k_{\{a_0\}}
\]
has zero one-sided interleaving distance from \(0\): for every
\(\varepsilon>0\), take both interleaving maps to be zero; the required
structure map \(S\to T_{2\varepsilon}S\) is already zero.  Nevertheless
\[
 S\neq0,\qquad R\Gamma(S)=\mathbb k.                       \tag{3}
\]
Thus zero interleaving size does not remove the exact-action cohomology
appearing in Asano--Ike Remark 4.2.

## Geometric countermodel

Let \(h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\) be quadratic near \(0\) and set
\[
 H_\varepsilon(z)=\varepsilon^2h(z/\varepsilon).
\]
Its Hofer norm is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), but on a ball of radius
\(c\varepsilon\) its time-one flow has a fixed non-trivial linearized
rotation germ.  The associated local Morse/Maslov object is independent of
\(\varepsilon\).  Hofer amplitude controls persistence length, not a
derivative, microlocal germ, or endpoint microstalk.

## The two precise categorical failures

First, smooth GKS locality is not presently known to pass through the
metric completion defining a Hamiltonian-homeomorphism kernel.  The
completion theorem controls objects.  It does not supply compatible local
continuation morphisms or show that their cones vanish after taking the
telescope.

Second, even if that locality is granted, the object
\[
 W=\mu hom(F_0,QF_0)|_{\rho^{-1}\Delta_0}
\]
is an ordinary sheaf on a subset of a cotangent bundle.  It has not been
shown to be limit constructible in a Tamarkin category.  Asano--Ike's
zero-distance rigidity therefore does not apply.  Passing to the observable
Tamarkin quotient would discard the skyscraper in (3), but Remark 4.2 asks
for the ordinary derived global sections that the quotient forgets.

## Correct conditional theorem

The cut-off argument would prove persistent diagonal locality if one could
establish both:

1. \(C^0\)-locality for the completed GKS kernels on a fixed conic
   neighborhood of the diagonal; and
2. zero-distance rigidity, or an equivalent no-ephemeral theorem, for the
   restricted diagonal \(\mu hom\).

Equivalently, for a telescope
\[
 F_C\simeq\operatorname*{hocolim}_n
 T_{-\varepsilon_{\geq n}}F_{C_n},
\]
one must show that restriction to the collapsed diagonal commutes with the
relevant derived inverse limit and that the cross-stage tower is pro-zero at
every \(a\notin\pi\mathbb Z\).  This is a Milnor-boundary theorem, not a
Hofer estimate.

## Verdict

The cut-off geometry is sound and identifies the missing class as
zero-lifetime, exact-action data.  It does not kill that class.  Any proof
which says only “the diagonal germ is arbitrarily Hofer-small” has not proved
Asano--Ike's cohomological vanishing.
