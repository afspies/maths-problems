Four-dimensional Mahler volume conjecture

A proof-first first attack: terminal polytopes and a sharp infinite-family theorem

partial results problems/mahler-volume-4d 23 July 2026
Abstract We audit the new three-dimensional shadow-flow proof, repair its dimension-specific persistence paragraph for dimension four, derive a necessary terminal-incidence inequality, classify simple, simplicial, and pyramid subclasses, and prove the sharp Mahler inequality for every four-dimensional pyramid. The general 4D conjecture remains open.

1 · Sharp result for all 4-pyramids

Theorem 1. Every four-dimensional pyramid P satisfies |P| |(P−s(P))°| ≥ 3125/576, with equality exactly for a 4-simplex.

Proof. Affinely write the pyramid as conv(K×{0},(0,1)), with s(K)=0. At z=(0,τ), the polar has height −1/τ≤r≤1/(1−τ) and horizontal section (1+rτ)K°. Since the centroid of K° is zero, the centroid–Santaló characterization reduces to the vertical integral; it vanishes at τ=1/(d+1). Integration gives

℘(pyrd K) = ((d+1)d+1/dd+2) ℘(K).

For d=4, the audited three-dimensional Mahler theorem gives

℘(P) ≥ (55/46) (44/(3!)2) = 3125/576.

Equality in dimension three forces the base to be a tetrahedron.

2 · Terminal-polytope bridge

For a facet F, admissibility is the family of exact equations λTα|F=0 over all affine dependences λ of its vertices. Choosing a direction parallel to a largest facet gives, for every pair-terminal 4-polytope,

2f03 ≥ 5(f0+f3)−10.

The supporting flag identity is f03−f02+2f2=2f3.

Theorem 2. A terminal simplicial 4-polytope is a simplex. A pair-terminal simple 4-polytope is a simplex. A pair-terminal 4-pyramid is a simplex.

For pyramids the key is exact descent: A(u,0)(pyr Q) ≅ Au(Q)⊕ℝ. The polar is again a pyramid over a dual 3-polytope, so the audited 3D dual-pair count forces a tetrahedral base.

3 · Source audit

The Meyer–Reisner convexity/rigidity theorem, volume-affinity, compactness, and the minimizer/polar chain are dimension-independent. The printed Chen–Li–Xi–Xu persistence proof follows intersections of two and three facets and is specifically 3D. In 4D, choose four independent active moved facets: their limiting intersection and the moved old vertex give a dimension-free replacement. The terminal classification—not the analytic reduction—is the remaining global obstacle.

5 · Limits and next route

Generic rigidity is too weak: the cube is rigid generically but not in facet-parallel directions. Incidence does not determine affine-dependence coefficients, and the present inequality leaves non-simple, non-simplicial, non-pyramidal types. The next proof campaign should test descent under wedges or truncations and seek rank gains from overlaps among facet constraints.
