Four-dimensional Kakeya: extraction frontier

Exact union, covering, and quadratic-degeneracy theorems

partial-results · bridge B go · 24 July 2026 · problems/kakeya-r4

Abstract. The full R⁴ Kakeya conjecture remains open. Harmonically transverse ruled quadratic stacks have dense shaded union at least λ²/log(1/δ). A fixed all-scale continuum stack satisfies a scale-sensitive incidence inequality, giving Hausdorff dimension four for that structured subclass. Exact countermodels show why small union does not itself extract such carriers, and a normal-form theorem isolates the remaining degenerate quadratic branch. No new general or sticky dimension bound is claimed.

Main theorem

Harmonic transverse-stack theorem. Let M≤Cδ−1 uniformly regular ruled quadric patches carry dense λ-shadings. If every double-overlap region satisfies

|∇Pi∧∇Pj| ≥c|i−j|/M,

then

|⋃Ui| ≥ cλ²Mδ / (1+MδHM−1). For M≈δ−1, this is cλ²/log(2+M).

Proof mechanism

Reach and the dense line sweep give |Ui|≥cλδ. Coarea on the rank-two locus, together with Crofton–Bézout, gives

|Ui∩Uj| ≤Cδ²M/|i−j|.

The pairwise losses sum to the harmonic number. The exact second moment then proves the displayed union bound. A weighted sparse form is also proved.

Fixed-stack Hausdorff theorem

Scale-sensitive incidence criterion. For one fixed measurable segment family, let A(V) be the longitudinal incidence mass lying in a measurable set V. If for every small dyadic r,

|NCr(V)| ≥ cA(V)²/L(r),

and Σr≤r₀r4−sL(r)→0 for every s<4, then Hs(K)=∞ and dimHK=4.

A fixed continuum harmonic stack supplies L(r)=1+log(1/r). More generally, retaining a fraction b(r)=ro(1) of every cover group’s incidence mass in one admissible chart is sufficient. This cover-by-cover qualifier is essential: unrelated stacks extracted from the whole union at each scale do not control arbitrary Hausdorff covers.

Explicit nonvacuous family

Put

q0=x1²+x2²−x3²−x4²,   q1=2x1x3+x3²,   Ps=q0+sq1−1.

Every member has split signature and inherits the exact SL2(R) line sweep. The combined carrier/direction chart has rational seed determinant 3/16, while

∇Ps∧∇Ps′ =(s′−s)∇q0∧∇q1.

Compact restriction yields the theorem’s uniform hypotheses. Fixed all-scale families covered by finitely many such direction charts have Hausdorff dimension four. This smooth explicit sweep actually has positive measure locally by the rank-four inverse function theorem; it is a nonvacuity model, not a delicate measure-zero construction.

Why small union does not extract carriers

If A is total shaded incidence and V is union volume, the multiplicity function satisfies ∫m²≥A²/V, and a high-multiplicity level carries a fixed fraction of A. This output contains no polynomial or ruling.

An exact grid of M≈δ−1 transverse hyperplane slabs gives distributed overlap q≈Mδ≈1: every tube crosses every slab for length Θ(δ), but no tube has assigned overlap λ≫δ with any slab. A valid extraction theorem must therefore produce assigned overlap, excess q−Mδ, or a geometric rule excluding ordinary crossings.

Inverse carrier energy. Once M genuine quadratic patches with |Ui|≈λδ are supplied,

|⋃Ui| ≥ cM²λ²δ² /(Mδ+Σi<jmin(δ,δ²/κij)).

Thus a small carrier union forces large inverse-normal-Jacobian energy. Classifying that energy is the missing theorem.

Degenerate quadratic branch

Regular irreducible nonlinear Hessian rank at most two, and regular central rank three, are pointwise 2-plany. Affine or reducible hyperplanes and conical spines are separate outputs. The genuine smooth ruled nonlinear exception is the indefinite rank-three paraboloid.

For Ps=z−y1y2−sy3²,

|∇Ps∧∇Pt| =2|s−t||x3||∇Ps∧e3|.

Away from |x3|≤ρ the transverse union estimate loses a real factor ρ; inside the slab the directions enter a weakly 2-plany alternative. This is an exact stress model, not yet a scale-loss-free rigidity theorem.
