# Literature audit — 2026-07-24

This file separates results that are often conflated. The audit used the
primary papers and the published journal page. “Current” means checked through
24 July 2026; a search for a later four-dimensional sticky improvement found
none.

## General Euclidean Kakeya in R⁴

- The full conjecture remains open.
- Corrected Katz–Zahl arXiv v3 proves a Kakeya maximal inequality at
  `d = 3 + (2195737-13925 sqrt(17665))/6959096 ≥ 3.049` and, by a separate
  X-ray argument, the stronger Hausdorff bound
  `3 + (sqrt(17665)-97)/600 > 3.059` (Theorem 2 of arXiv:1902.00989v3).
  The latter is the current general Hausdorff benchmark found in the audit.
  The publisher abstract still advertises the pre-correction maximal
  exponent; no publisher correction notice was found.
- The same number is automatically a lower bound for lower and upper
  Minkowski dimension because Hausdorff dimension is no larger than either.
  This is not a separate proof of full Minkowski dimension.
- Borges–Chan–Chen–Liu–Xi–Zhan (arXiv:2511.22824v1) prove the current Kakeya
  maximal estimate at
  `d₀ = (159+sqrt(145))/56 ≈ 3.0543`. It improves the corrected
  Katz–Zahl *maximal estimate* `3.049`, but is weaker than the `3.059`
  Hausdorff consequence obtained by Katz–Zahl using an X-ray estimate.
  Their final Remark explicitly says their self-improvement does not improve
  the Hausdorff lower bound. Therefore `3.0543` is not the general Hausdorff
  benchmark.

## Sticky Euclidean Kakeya in R⁴

- Rai Choudhuri, Theorem 1.2 / Theorem 3.10, proves that every sticky Kakeya
  set in R⁴ has Hausdorff dimension at least `13/4 = 3.25`.
- The journal record is Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 42 (2026), no. 4, 1227–1256,
  DOI 10.4171/RMI/1621; accepted 20 March 2026 and published 5 May 2026.
- Again, `13/4` is consequently also a lower bound for both Minkowski
  dimensions of the same sets. The paper does not prove full sticky dimension
  four in R⁴.
- No later strict improvement was found through 24 July 2026.
  An official-arXiv search found no post-May-2026 improvement; in particular,
  arXiv:2607.14824 is a construction/upper-neighborhood result, not a lower
  bound.

## Three dimensions (context, not an R⁴ bound)

- Wang–Zahl (arXiv:2210.09581) prove the sticky Kakeya conjecture in R³:
  sticky sets have Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension three.
- Wang–Zahl (arXiv:2502.17655) prove the full Kakeya conjecture in R³:
  every Kakeya set has Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension three.
- Guth (arXiv:2508.05475) gives a detailed outline of the full-to-sticky
  reduction and explicitly says the direct convex-union theorem fails in
  dimensions at least four.

## Restricted and finite-field results (not benchmarks for general R⁴)

- Fraser–Yang (arXiv:2505.05709) study Kakeya configurations whose segment
  midpoints lie in a fixed set of packing dimension at most `s`. In R⁴ they
  obtain
  `dim_H K ≥ max(19/5 - 3s/5, 4-s)`.
  This is a restricted hypothesis, not a general or sticky bound.
- Dvir's finite-field Kakeya theorem gives full finite-field dimension:
  a Kakeya set in `F_q^n` has cardinality at least `c_n q^n`. It is a distinct
  setting and does not prove the Euclidean R⁴ conjecture.

## Input from arXiv:2511.22824 relevant to bridge A

Lemma 6.1 as quoted there produces a scale `rho`, uniform coarse and fine
multiplicities, rho-planiness of fine bushes, and simultaneous trilinear
transversality inside the plank. Its discussion says that taking account of
“graininess inside the shaded rho-tubes could potentially provide sharper
control” than its multiplicity product bound. This is explicitly a proposed
improvement, not a proved grain inverse theorem. The paper supplies useful
balanced multiplicity bookkeeping but no theorem that turns near equality
into a ruled algebraic grain.

## Polynomial Wolff and low-degree carrier results

- Katz–Rogers (arXiv:1802.09094), Theorem 1.1, prove that
  direction-separated delta-tubes satisfy the bounded-complexity
  semialgebraic polynomial Wolff axiom up to `delta^-epsilon`. In R⁴:

  `#{T: |T intersect S|≥lambda|T|}
   ≤ C_epsilon |S| delta^(-3-epsilon) lambda^-4`.

  Thus degree-two QW2 is known for the direction-separated Kakeya regime; it
  is not a new conjectural axiom. It can fail for essentially-distinct
  families with repeated directions, such as the thinned/copied quadric
  obstruction.
- Zahl (arXiv:1801.05106) proves that only
  `O(delta^(-2-epsilon))` direction-separated tubes can lie near any fixed
  low-degree hypersurface in R⁴. The second-session conditioned quadratic
  lemma is an elementary, lambda-explicit refinement for uniformly
  nondegenerate quadrics, not the first direction-capacity theorem.

These results relocate Bridge B's missing input: it is not single-grain
nonconcentration for a direction-separated family, but an inner/outer union
theorem for the roughly `delta^-1` grains needed to account for all
directions, together with extraction of an organized grain family from a
small union.
